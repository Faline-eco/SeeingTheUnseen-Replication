# Example detections

What each of the six representations detects on the same held-out frames, written
as individual images so they can be dropped into a paper one at a time or read on
their own.

All examples come from **flight 70, thermal**, which sits in **fold 1's validation
split** — every image is therefore drawn with a head that never saw that frame in
training. Four frames are shown, chosen as the most occluded in the flight: 15
annotated animals, 11 of them invisible in the central view.

Generated by `scripts/make_example_figures.py`.

## How to read a figure

| box | meaning |
|---|---|
| **solid** outline | animal visible in the central view |
| **dashed** outline | animal **invisible centrally**, reachable only through the aperture |
| green | found by this representation at IoU ≥ 0.5 |
| red | missed |

Only ground truth is drawn. Pass `--show-fp` to add detections that match no
annotation, in dotted yellow; they are off by default because they crowd the panels
and the count is in [`notes_thermal.json`](notes_thermal.json) anyway.

Each box is a 4 pt coloured stroke over a black stroke 2 pt wider, leaving 1 pt of
black on either side. The halo is what makes the colours work: without it a green
box sitting on a green false-colour region is nearly invisible, and the outlines
disappear against bright animals and dark canopy in the sensor panels. It is drawn
as a matplotlib path effect rather than a second rectangle, so it follows the same
path and the dashes stay in phase.

Both the stroke width and the dash pattern were tuned rather than guessed.
matplotlib scales the dash pattern by line width, and these boxes are only about
30 px a side: a coarse pattern puts a single dash on each edge and reads as corner
brackets, while too fine a pattern lets the black end-cap of each dash swallow the
colour. `--lw` and `--halo` expose both.

Box geometry is the annotation's, unscaled. The boxes are small because the animals
are small.

Every figure of a given frame shows the **same ground at the same crop**, so boxes
line up across representations and the six can be compared directly. Nothing is
burnt into the images — no titles, no legends — so captions belong wherever they
are used.

## The six representations

| filename suffix | cell | what is shown |
|---|---|---|
| `raw-single` | `realortho_single` | the orthorectified central frame, 2048² |
| `raw-multi` | `realalfs_multi` | the 31-view ALFS integral, 2048² |
| `dinov3-single` | `embed_single` | DINOv3 features of the central view |
| `dinov3-multi` | `embed_multi` | DINOv3 features integrated across the aperture |
| `vjepa-single` | `vjepa_single` | V-JEPA features of the central view |
| `vjepa-multi` | `vjepa_multi` | V-JEPA features of the aperture clip |

The two sensor rows are displayed at the **native 2048² render resolution**. The
detector itself always runs on the 128×128 cell it was trained on — boxes are in
normalised coordinates, so they map to either — but showing a 128×128 cell where a
2048px render exists would throw away everything a reader needs to judge the
imagery.

The four embedding rows have no higher-resolution form: 128×128 **is** the grid,
and an animal spans roughly two cells on it. They are false colour over the three
leading principal components of that frame's own feature grid, after a blurred copy
of each channel has been subtracted — see below.

## Reading the embedding figures honestly

A principal-component view shows what dominates **variance**, not what the detector
uses, and in these features the leading components are dominated by **absolute
position** rather than content. The multi-view cells are worse than the single-view
ones for a specific reason: averaging 31 views cancels content variation, while the
positional component is common to every view and survives intact. Projected
directly, a multi-view cell is close to a featureless colour ramp.

The figures therefore subtract a blurred copy of each channel before the projection
— two passes of a separable box blur, kernel 17 — which suppresses the low-frequency
positional part and lets the remaining structure show. **This is a display transform
only.** The detector reads the untouched grid, so every box on these figures comes
from the real input, and the counts below are identical with and without it.

What it reveals is worth looking at: with the ramp removed, `dinov3-single` shows
the canopy as discrete crowns, and `dinov3-multi` shows the same structure
attenuated and drawn out — which is the mechanism the multi-view axis exploits,
visible in the features themselves.

Pass `--viz pca` to reproduce the untransformed projection. Two alternatives were
tried and rejected: dropping the first principal component (helps less, and which
component carries position is not fixed), and rendering the INSID3-debiased cells
that already exist on disk (works well, but those are a *different* input from the
one the plotted detections come from).

The animals themselves are still mostly not resolvable by eye at 128×128 — the
transform makes the field legible, not the targets.

### Why V-JEPA is tiled and DINOv3 is not

The V-JEPA figures show an obvious **grid**, roughly every 24 cells. It is an
artefact of how the two encoders have to be run, not a property of the scene:

- **DINOv3** ViT-H+/16 takes the whole 2048² frame in one forward pass, giving
  2048/16 = **128×128 patches directly**. One attention context, one continuous
  positional embedding, no seams — but a smooth global positional gradient that
  dominates the leading components. That gradient is low-frequency, so the display
  high-pass removes it, which is why the `dinov3-*` figures show content rather than
  a ramp.
- **V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-B-384** has a fixed 384px input. A 2048² frame must be cut into
  **6×6 = 36 tiles** (offsets 0, 384, 768, 1152, 1536, and 1664 — the last pulled
  back so every tile is real image rather than padding, which makes the last row
  and column overlap by 256px and be averaged). Each tile is encoded
  **independently**, produces 384/16 = 24×24 cells, and the tiles are stitched into
  the same 128×128 grid.

Tokens at a tile border see a truncated neighbourhood and carry an extreme
positional code, so their features differ systematically from interior tokens. That
systematic offset is what paints the grid in the false-colour projection, and it is
also why the last row and column look different again — those are the averaged
overlap. Unlike the DINOv3 ramp, the seams are **high-frequency**, so the display
high-pass does not remove them and in fact makes them more prominent. That is the
honest trade: the V-JEPA panels now show real content — the drainage line and, in
places, the animals themselves as small dark spots — at the cost of louder seams.

Worth stating plainly: the grid is **visible but apparently not what limits
accuracy**. It is exactly the structure the INSID3 positional-debias probe was
built to remove, and removing it reached p < 0.05 in 5 of 64 comparisons against
3.2 expected by chance, with none surviving Bonferroni correction. So the seams should be read as a rendering
consequence of the tiling, not as evidence about detector quality, and a reader
should not mistake the grid for scene structure.

## The frames

Counts are `found / annotated (hidden found / hidden annotated)` at conf 0.3,
IoU 0.5, fold 1, best seed by mAP50-95. Raw per-cell numbers including false
positives are in [`notes_thermal.json`](notes_thermal.json).

### `70_001360` — 4 animals, 3 hidden

The single view is broken up by dark canopy patches; only the one centrally visible
animal is found. The ALFS integral dissolves that canopy texture into a smooth
background and one hidden animal becomes a compact bright shape, recovering it.
DINOv3 over the aperture does best here at 3/4.

| representation | found |
|---|---|
| `raw-single` | 1/4 (0/3 hidden) |
| `raw-multi` | 2/4 (1/3) |
| `dinov3-single` | 2/4 (1/3) |
| `dinov3-multi` | **3/4 (2/3)** |
| `vjepa-single` | 1/4 (0/3) |
| `vjepa-multi` | 1/4 (0/3) |

### `70_001370` — 4 animals, 3 hidden

Ten frames later, essentially the same scene from a slightly shifted viewpoint —
useful as a check that the effect is not one lucky frame. Every representation
lands at 2/4 except `dinov3-single` at 1/4. The single ortho view scores 2/4 here
but with two false positives; the ALFS integral reaches the same 2/4 with none.
This frame is the honest counterexample in the set: aperture does not help every
frame.

| representation | found |
|---|---|
| `raw-single` | 2/4 (1/3), 2 false positives |
| `raw-multi` | 2/4 (1/3), 0 false positives |
| `dinov3-single` | 1/4 (0/3) |
| `dinov3-multi` | 2/4 (1/3) |
| `vjepa-single` | 2/4 (1/3) |
| `vjepa-multi` | 2/4 (1/3) |

### `70_001640` — 4 animals, 3 hidden

The strongest case in the set. The single view finds one animal of four; DINOv3
over the aperture finds **all four, including all three hidden**. Both multi-view
sensor and multi-view embedding roughly triple the single-view result.

`raw-multi` also produces two detections that match no annotation, visible with
`--show-fp`. One is a second detection beside the visible animal; the other sits on
a clear animal-shaped blob at the upper left, around normalised (0.56, 0.35). Both
count as false positives because the annotation is the reference — but the second is
plausibly a real animal the annotation misses, which is a limitation of the
evaluation rather than of the detector.

| representation | found |
|---|---|
| `raw-single` | 1/4 (0/3) |
| `raw-multi` | 3/4 (2/3) |
| `dinov3-single` | 1/4 (1/3) |
| `dinov3-multi` | **4/4 (3/3)** |
| `vjepa-single` | 1/4 (1/3) |
| `vjepa-multi` | 3/4 (2/3) |

### `70_001350` — 3 animals, 2 hidden

The clearest illustration of what the aperture does to the *imagery*, independent
of any detector. In `raw-single` all three animals are ragged blobs mixed into
foliage speckle. In `raw-multi` the foliage clutter is smeared away by the
integration and each animal resolves into a compact, clearly animal-shaped bright
region — this is the resolution gain, not merely an invisible-to-visible flip.

The instructive part is that the pixel-space head still only finds 1/3 on that
cleaner image, while `dinov3-multi` finds **3/3 including both hidden animals**.
Cleaner imagery and better detection are not the same thing.

| representation | found |
|---|---|
| `raw-single` | 0/3 (0/2) |
| `raw-multi` | 1/3 (0/2) |
| `dinov3-single` | 2/3 (1/2) |
| `dinov3-multi` | **3/3 (2/2)** |
| `vjepa-single` | 2/3 (1/2) |
| `vjepa-multi` | 2/3 (1/2) |

## What the set shows

The four frames hold 11 hidden animals, so each of the three encoder families has
11 chances and the three together have 33. The single-view arms find **7 of 33**;
the multi-view arms find **16 of 33** — raw 1→4, DINOv3 3→8, V-JEPA 3→4. Counting
all animals rather than only hidden ones, multi-view is at least as good as its
single-view counterpart in **every one of the twelve frame × family pairings**, and
never worse.

These are illustrations, not evidence. The claim itself rests on the
cross-validated aggregate — 5 scene-level folds × 3 seeds — where the aperture
roughly doubles hidden recall in pixel space while leaving visible recall
unchanged. Four frames of one flight cannot establish that; they can only show what
it looks like.

## Regenerating

```bash
python scripts/make_example_figures.py \
  --modality thermal --flight 70 --fold 1 \
  --stems 70_001360,70_001370,70_001640,70_001350 \
  --out figures/examples
```

The frames must belong to the chosen fold's **validation** split. A frame drawn
with a head that trained on it is not evidence of anything, so
`--fold` and `--flight` have to agree: flight 70 is in fold 1.

RGB examples are not included — flight 70 was selected on its thermal occlusion.
The same command with `--modality rgb` produces them if wanted.
